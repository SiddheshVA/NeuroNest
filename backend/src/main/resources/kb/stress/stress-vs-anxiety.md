# Stress and anxiety - how they differ

## Overview
Stress and anxiety feel similar and often show up together, but they are not quite the same.

**Stress** is usually linked to something outside the person, like an upcoming test or a conflict with a friend. It tends to ease once the situation is over. It can even be helpful in small amounts, for example by motivating someone to prepare.

**Anxiety** is more of an inner reaction. It can continue even when there is no clear threat in front of someone. It often feels like ongoing worry, unease or dread.

Both can affect the body and mind in similar ways, such as poor sleep, tension, headaches, irritability and trouble focusing.

## What you can try
- Ask: "Is there a specific thing I am reacting to right now?" If yes, practical problem-solving may help.
- If the worry keeps going without a clear cause, calming skills like slow breathing, grounding or a short mindfulness exercise may help settle the body.
- Notice patterns: when the feeling starts, how long it lasts, and what helps.
- Stay connected with people who help you cope in a positive way.

## Safety and limitations
This document explains general differences. It cannot determine whether someone has an anxiety disorder or any other condition. Only a qualified professional can assess that.

## When professional support may help
If worry or unease does not go away, keeps growing, or starts to interfere with daily life, sleep or relationships, talking with a doctor or mental health professional is a good next step.
