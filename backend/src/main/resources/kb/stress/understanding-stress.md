# Understanding stress

## Overview
Stress is how the body and mind respond to a demand or pressure from outside, such as a heavy workload, an exam, an argument, an illness or a big life change. Everyone experiences stress at some point. It can come from a single event or from something that keeps happening over weeks or months.

Stress is not always negative. A little pressure can help someone focus or meet a deadline. When stress is intense or lasts a long time, though, it can start to affect sleep, mood, concentration and physical health.

## Common signs people notice
- Feeling tense, irritable or on edge
- Trouble falling or staying asleep
- Headaches, muscle tension or an upset stomach
- Racing thoughts or difficulty concentrating
- Changes in appetite or energy

These are common experiences, not a diagnosis. Different people notice different signs.

## What you can try
- Name what is causing the pressure. Writing down the main stressors can make them feel more manageable.
- Break big tasks into small, concrete steps.
- Use a short calming practice, such as slow breathing or a brief walk.
- Keep basic routines going: regular meals, sleep and movement.
- Talk to someone you trust about how you are feeling.

## Safety and limitations
This information is general education about stress. It cannot tell whether what someone is experiencing is stress or something else, and it is not a substitute for advice from a health professional.

## When professional support may help
It may be worth reaching out to a doctor, counsellor or mental health professional if stress lasts for weeks, keeps getting worse, or makes it hard to manage studies, work, relationships or daily tasks.
