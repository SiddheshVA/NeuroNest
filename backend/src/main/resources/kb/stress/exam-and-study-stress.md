# Exam and study stress

## Overview
Exams, deadlines and pressure to perform are some of the most common sources of stress for students. Some nervousness before an exam is normal and can even help with focus. Problems tend to arise when the pressure feels constant, sleep suffers, or worry makes it hard to study at all.

## What you can try
- **Plan in small blocks.** Study in focused sessions (for example 25-45 minutes) with short breaks.
- **Make a realistic timetable** and include rest, meals and sleep in it.
- **Notice "what if" thoughts.** When a thought like "I will fail everything" shows up, name it as a thought rather than a fact, then bring attention back to the next small task.
- **Use a 1-minute reset** before studying or entering an exam: slow your breathing, feel your feet on the floor, and notice three things around you.
- **Keep connected.** Studying with a friend or talking about worries can reduce the sense of carrying it alone.
- **Be kind to yourself.** Speak to yourself the way you would to a friend under pressure.

## Safety and limitations
These tips are for everyday study pressure. They are not a treatment for anxiety or other conditions and cannot guarantee exam results.

## When professional support may help
If exam stress leads to panic, ongoing sleep problems, avoiding classes, or feeling hopeless, it may help to contact a college counsellor, a doctor or a mental health professional.
