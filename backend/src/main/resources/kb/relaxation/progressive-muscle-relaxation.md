# Progressive muscle relaxation

## Overview
Progressive muscle relaxation (PMR) involves tensing a group of muscles, then releasing that tension and noticing the difference. Working through the body this way can ease physical tension that builds up during stress. It is often combined with slow breathing or guided imagery and is a common part of bedtime routines.

## How to do it (about 10 minutes)
1. Sit or lie down comfortably. Take a few slow breaths.
2. Start with your feet. Curl your toes and tense your feet gently for about 5 seconds.
3. Release, and relax for 10-15 seconds. Notice how the muscles feel as they soften.
4. Move up the body, one area at a time:
   - calves
   - thighs
   - buttocks
   - stomach
   - hands (make fists)
   - arms
   - shoulders (raise toward ears)
   - face (scrunch, then release)
5. Finish with a few slow breaths, noticing your whole body.

## Tips
- Tense firmly but gently. It should never hurt.
- A shorter version can focus only on shoulders, hands and jaw.

## Safety and limitations
PMR is generally considered safe for healthy people. Skip or go very gently with any area that is injured or painful, and talk to a doctor first if you have muscle, joint or other health problems. It is not a treatment for any medical or mental health condition.

## When professional support may help
If tension, pain or stress continue despite regular practice, talk to a doctor or mental health professional.
