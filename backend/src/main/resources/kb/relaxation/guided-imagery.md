# Guided imagery

## Overview
Guided imagery is a relaxation technique where you focus on calm, pleasant images in your mind, using as many senses as possible. It can help shift attention away from stress and support the body's relaxation response. It is sometimes combined with slow breathing or progressive muscle relaxation.

## How to do it (5-10 minutes)
1. Sit or lie comfortably and close your eyes, or rest your gaze on one spot.
2. Take a few slow breaths.
3. Picture a place where you feel calm and safe. It could be real or imagined: a beach, a garden, a quiet room.
4. Explore it with your senses:
   - What do you see? Colours, light, shapes.
   - What do you hear? Waves, birds, silence.
   - What do you feel? Warm sun, a soft breeze, grass under your feet.
   - What can you smell?
5. Stay there for a few minutes, breathing slowly.
6. When ready, take a deeper breath, notice the room around you, and open your eyes.

## Tips
- Recorded or app-based guidance can help when starting out.
- The same place can become a quick "mental retreat" you return to.

## Safety and limitations
Guided imagery is generally considered safe for healthy people. If an image brings up unpleasant memories or feelings, open your eyes and choose a different, neutral image or a grounding exercise. It is not a substitute for medical or psychological care.

## When professional support may help
If relaxation practices often bring up distressing memories, or if stress remains hard to manage, consider talking with a mental health professional.
