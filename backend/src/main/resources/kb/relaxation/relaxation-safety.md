# Relaxation techniques - safety and limits

## Overview
Relaxation techniques such as deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation and guided imagery are generally considered safe for healthy people. Still, it helps to know their limits so they are used in a helpful way.

## What to keep in mind
- **Rare reactions:** There have been rare reports that certain relaxation techniques may cause or worsen symptoms in people with epilepsy, certain psychiatric conditions, or a history of abuse or trauma.
- **Not a replacement for care:** Relaxation techniques should not replace conventional medical care or be used to put off seeing a doctor about a health problem.
- **Evidence varies:** Research supports relaxation as part of stress management and healthy sleep habits, but evidence for treating specific health conditions is limited.
- **Talk therapy may be more effective for some concerns:** For generalised anxiety, studies suggest cognitive behavioural therapy may be more effective over the long term than relaxation alone.

## Practical guidance
- Stop any practice that makes you feel worse, more anxious or distressed.
- Try a different technique; what suits one person may not suit another.
- If you have a serious physical or mental health condition, discuss relaxation techniques with your health care provider.

## When professional support may help
If you are dealing with ongoing distress, trauma-related symptoms, or a health condition, a professional can help choose approaches that are safe and effective for you.

## Safety and limitations
This document summarises general research findings. It cannot predict how any technique will affect a particular person and is not medical advice.
