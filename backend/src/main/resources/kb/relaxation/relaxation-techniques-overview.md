# Relaxation techniques - an overview

## Overview
Relaxation techniques are practices that help bring about the body's relaxation response: slower breathing, lower blood pressure, a slower heart rate and a sense of calm. This is the opposite of the stress response, where the body releases hormones that prepare it to fight or flee.

## Common types
- **Deep breathing exercises:** slowing and deepening the breath.
- **Progressive muscle relaxation:** tensing and then releasing muscle groups one at a time.
- **Guided imagery:** focusing on calm, pleasant images in the mind.
- **Autogenic training:** using self-suggestions to focus on feelings of warmth, heaviness and relaxation in the body.
- **Self-hypnosis** and **biofeedback-assisted relaxation** (which uses devices to show body signals like muscle tension).

Mindfulness, yoga and tai chi are related practices that often include relaxation.

## Getting the most from them
- Relaxation is a skill; regular, frequent practice matters.
- Most techniques can be learned and practised on your own after brief instruction.
- They seem to work best combined with healthy habits like good nutrition, regular exercise and strong social support.

## Safety and limitations
Relaxation techniques are generally considered safe for healthy people, but there is limited evidence of benefit for specific health conditions. They should not replace conventional care or delay seeing a doctor.

## When professional support may help
If you are considering relaxation techniques for a specific health condition, talk to your health care provider. For ongoing stress or anxiety, a mental health professional can help.
