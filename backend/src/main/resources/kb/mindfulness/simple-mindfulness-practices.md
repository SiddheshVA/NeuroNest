# Simple everyday mindfulness practices

## Overview
Mindfulness does not need special equipment or long sessions. WHO's stress guide describes a skill it calls **engaging**: giving full attention to what you are doing, instead of being pulled away by stressful thoughts. Short practices like these can fit into a normal day.

## What you can try
**One-minute breath focus**
Sit comfortably. Notice the breath moving in and out. When your mind wanders, gently return to the breath.

**Mindful sip or bite**
With your next drink or snack, slow down. Notice the temperature, taste, smell and texture before you swallow.

**Mindful walking**
As you walk, notice the feeling of each foot touching the ground, the movement of your arms, and the sounds around you.

**Engaging in a task**
Pick an everyday activity, such as washing your hands, and use all your senses: the sound of the water, how it feels, the smell of soap.

**Noticing five things**
Pause and silently name five things you can see right now, then three you can hear.

## Tips
- Consistency matters more than length. A few minutes daily is a good start.
- Expect your mind to wander. Returning attention is the skill being built.

## Safety and limitations
These practices are generally low-risk. If one brings up strong distress, stop and try something more active or grounding. They are not a treatment for any condition.

## When professional support may help
If difficult feelings persist or interfere with daily life, consider talking with a counsellor or mental health professional.
