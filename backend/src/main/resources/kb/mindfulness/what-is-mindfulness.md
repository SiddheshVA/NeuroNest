# What mindfulness and meditation are

## Overview
Meditation is a practice with a very long history, and many techniques began in Eastern traditions. Some forms involve focusing attention on one thing, like the breath, a sensation, or a repeated word or phrase.

**Mindfulness** is a related idea: paying attention to what is happening in the present moment, inside and around you, without judging it as good or bad. It can be practised formally (sitting quietly for a set time) or informally (bringing full attention to everyday activities like eating, walking or washing up).

Programs that teach mindfulness sometimes combine it with other activities, such as gentle movement or group discussion.

## What you can try
- Start small: one to five minutes is enough to begin.
- Choose an anchor, such as your breath or the feeling of your feet on the floor.
- When your mind wanders (it will), notice where it went, and gently bring it back. This "coming back" is the practice, not a failure.
- Try one mindful daily activity: eat a meal or drink a cup of tea while giving it your full attention.

## Safety and limitations
Meditation and mindfulness usually carry few risks, but some people do have unpleasant experiences, such as increased anxiety or difficult memories coming up. It is okay to stop, open your eyes, or switch to a more active practice like walking. Mindfulness is not a replacement for medical or psychological care.

## When professional support may help
If practice regularly brings up distressing feelings or memories, or if you are managing a mental health condition, consider exploring mindfulness with guidance from a qualified professional.
