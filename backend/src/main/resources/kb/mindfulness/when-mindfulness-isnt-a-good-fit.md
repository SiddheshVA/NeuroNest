# When mindfulness isn't a good fit

## Overview
Meditation and mindfulness are usually considered low-risk, and many people find them calming. However, they are not right for everyone at every moment. Some people notice more anxiety, uncomfortable body sensations, or upsetting memories and feelings when they sit quietly and turn attention inward.

This does not mean someone is "doing it wrong". It is a signal to adjust the practice or try something different.

## What you can try instead
- **Keep your eyes open** and focus on something outside you, such as sounds or objects in the room.
- **Shorten the practice** to a minute or less.
- **Choose movement-based options** such as mindful walking, stretching or gentle yoga.
- **Use grounding** (feet on the floor, naming objects around you) rather than focusing on the breath or body.
- **Focus on an activity** like drawing, cooking or listening to music.

## Safety and limitations
People with a history of trauma, or with certain mental or physical health conditions, may need practices to be adapted. Mindfulness is not a substitute for professional treatment, and no one should feel pushed to continue a practice that feels distressing.

## When professional support may help
If mindfulness or quiet time often brings up distressing memories, panic or a sense of unreality, it may help to explore this with a mental health professional who can suggest safer approaches.
