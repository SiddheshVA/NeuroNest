# Mindfulness - what the research says

## Overview
Interest in mindfulness has grown a lot, and researchers have studied it for many purposes. NCCIH summarises the current picture. Overall, results are encouraging in some areas and mixed in others.

## Key points
- **Anxiety and low mood:** Mindfulness-based practices may help some people with anxiety and depression.
- **Sleep:** Mindfulness meditation may reduce insomnia and improve sleep quality for some people.
- **Post-traumatic stress:** Some studies suggest meditation and mindfulness may reduce symptoms, including in veterans.
- **Substance use recovery:** Mindfulness-based approaches may support people recovering from substance use disorders.
- **Pain:** Results for pain have been mixed.
- **Risks:** Practices are usually considered low-risk, but some people do have negative experiences.

## What this means in practice
Mindfulness can be a useful tool for many people, but it is not a cure-all, and it works differently for different people. It tends to help most when practised regularly over time.

## Safety and limitations
Research findings describe groups of people and cannot promise a particular result for any one person. Mindfulness should not replace care from health professionals for any condition.

## When professional support may help
For ongoing anxiety, low mood, trauma symptoms or substance use concerns, a health professional can recommend approaches with strong evidence, which may include structured mindfulness-based programs.
