# How much sleep is enough

## Overview
Sleep needs change across life and vary between people, but there are general guidelines.

- **Adults:** about 7 to 9 hours a night. Sleeping more than 9 hours is not necessarily harmful, and may be helpful for young adults, people catching up on lost sleep, or people who are unwell.
- **Teens (13-18):** about 8 to 10 hours a day.
- **Children (6-12):** about 9 to 12 hours a day.

## Sleep debt
When someone regularly sleeps less than they need, the missing hours add up. This is often called "sleep debt". For example, losing two hours a night adds up to about fourteen hours over a week. Naps and long weekend lie-ins can help someone feel better, but they do not fully replace regular night-time sleep, and big swings in sleep timing can upset the body's sleep-wake rhythm.

## What you can try
- Work backwards from your wake-up time to choose a bedtime that allows enough sleep.
- Keep sleep and wake times similar on weekdays and weekends.
- If you often sleep much longer on days off, it may be a sign you need more sleep during the week.

## Safety and limitations
These numbers are general guidance, not targets that apply to everyone. Individual needs vary, and this information cannot assess any sleep problem.

## When professional support may help
Speak to a doctor if you are regularly sleeping much more or much less than these ranges and feel unwell, very tired, or unable to function during the day.
