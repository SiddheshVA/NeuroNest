# Why sleep matters for wellbeing

## Overview
Sleep is not just "switching off". The brain is very active during sleep, and the work it does overnight supports how we think, feel and function the next day. When people regularly cut back on sleep, it can affect attention, reaction time, mood and physical health.

Sleep quality matters as much as the number of hours. Sleep moves through different stages during the night, and feeling rested depends on getting enough total sleep and enough of these stages.

## How poor sleep can show up
- Difficulty focusing or remembering things
- Feeling irritable, low or more easily stressed
- Slower reactions, which can be risky when driving
- Feeling tired even after a long time in bed

## What you can try
- Treat sleep as a priority rather than the first thing to cut when busy.
- Keep a simple sleep diary for one or two weeks: bedtime, wake time, how rested you feel, caffeine and screen use. Patterns often become clearer.
- See the documents "Healthy sleep habits" and "Relaxing before bed" for practical steps.

## Safety and limitations
This document gives general information. It cannot identify sleep disorders such as insomnia or sleep apnea, which need assessment by a health professional.

## When professional support may help
Talk to a doctor if poor sleep lasts for several weeks, if you snore loudly or stop breathing during sleep (as noticed by others), if you feel very sleepy during the day despite enough time in bed, or if sleep problems are affecting your daily life.
