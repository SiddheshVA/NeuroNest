# Healthy sleep habits

## Overview
Small daily habits can make it easier to fall asleep and wake up rested. NIH research points to a few key factors: good-quality, uninterrupted sleep; a regular bedtime and wake time; enough hours of sleep; and daylight exposure during the day.

## What you can try
**During the day**
- Go to bed and get up at about the same time every day.
- Get some natural daylight, ideally around 30 minutes.
- Be physically active, but try not to exercise hard in the 2-3 hours before bed.
- If you nap, keep it short and avoid napping late in the afternoon.
- Cut back on caffeine and nicotine, especially later in the day.

**In the evening**
- Avoid heavy meals and lots of drinks late at night.
- Avoid alcohol before bed; it can disrupt sleep later in the night.
- Wind down with something calm, such as reading, gentle stretching or a breathing exercise.
- Dim screens and lights in the hour before bed.

**Your sleep space**
- Keep the room dark, quiet and at a comfortable temperature.
- Use the bed mainly for sleep, not for work or long screen sessions.

## Safety and limitations
These are general habits. They may not resolve every sleep problem. Some medicines can affect sleep; do not stop or change a prescribed medicine without talking to a doctor.

## When professional support may help
If sleep problems continue for weeks despite good habits, or come with loud snoring, breathing pauses, or strong daytime sleepiness, a doctor can check for sleep conditions.
