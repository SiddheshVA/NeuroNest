# Relaxing before bed

## Overview
A busy mind and a tense body can make it hard to fall asleep. According to NCCIH, there is evidence that relaxation techniques used before bedtime, such as progressive muscle relaxation, guided imagery or deep breathing, can be helpful parts of a plan to improve sleep habits. Mindfulness practices may also help some people sleep better.

## A simple wind-down routine (10-15 minutes)
1. Turn off or put away screens.
2. Sit or lie comfortably and take a few slow breaths, letting each out-breath be a little longer.
3. Do a short body scan or progressive muscle relaxation: gently tense and release each muscle group from feet to face.
4. Picture a calm, safe place in detail: what you would see, hear and feel there.
5. If worries keep coming, jot them on paper with one small next step for tomorrow, then return to your breathing.

## If you cannot sleep
If you have been lying awake for a long time, some people find it helps to get up, do something quiet in dim light, and return to bed when sleepy.

## Safety and limitations
Relaxation techniques are generally considered safe for healthy people. Rarely, some techniques have been reported to bring up uncomfortable feelings in people with certain health conditions or a history of trauma. Stop if a practice feels distressing. These steps are not a treatment for insomnia or other sleep disorders.

## When professional support may help
If trouble sleeping continues most nights for several weeks, talk to a doctor. Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia is one approach professionals may suggest.
