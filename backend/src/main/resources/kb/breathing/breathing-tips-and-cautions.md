# Breathing exercises - tips and cautions

## Overview
Breathing exercises are one of the easiest relaxation skills to learn and use anywhere. Like other skills, they work best with regular practice, and they are most useful alongside other healthy habits such as good sleep, physical activity and social support.

## Tips for practice
- **Practise when calm first.** It is easier to use a skill under stress if it already feels familiar.
- **Keep it short and regular.** A few minutes once or twice a day is a good start.
- **Breathe gently.** Slow does not mean forced. Overly deep or fast breathing can cause dizziness.
- **Lengthen the out-breath.** Many people find a slightly longer out-breath feels most calming.
- **Pair it with a cue,** such as before a class, after waking, or at bedtime.

## Cautions
- Stop and breathe normally if you feel dizzy, light-headed, tingly or more anxious.
- People with heart or lung conditions, or who are pregnant, may want to check with a doctor before trying techniques involving breath-holding.
- If focusing on the breath makes you feel worse, try grounding with your senses instead.

## Safety and limitations
Breathing exercises are generally considered safe for healthy people but have limited evidence for treating specific health conditions. They should not replace medical care or delay seeing a doctor.

## When professional support may help
Seek medical help for unexplained breathlessness or chest pain. For stress or anxiety that stays high despite these skills, consider speaking with a mental health professional.
