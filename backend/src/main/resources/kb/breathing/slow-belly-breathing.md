# Slow belly breathing

## Overview
When we are stressed, the body's "fight-or-flight" response speeds up breathing and heart rate. Relaxation techniques aim to bring on the opposite: the relaxation response, which involves slower breathing, a calmer heart rate and a sense of ease. Deep breathing exercises are one of the simplest ways to do this, and most people can learn them with little instruction.

## How to do it
1. Sit or lie in a comfortable position. Place one hand on your chest and one on your belly.
2. Breathe in slowly through your nose for about 4 counts. Let your belly rise under your hand, while your chest stays fairly still.
3. Breathe out gently through your mouth or nose for about 4-6 counts, letting your belly fall.
4. Repeat for 2-5 minutes. Keep the breath gentle, not forced.

## Tips
- It can feel unusual at first. Like any skill, it gets easier with regular practice.
- Practising when you are already fairly calm makes it easier to use when stressed.
- It combines well with relaxing your shoulders and jaw.

## Safety and limitations
Breathing exercises are generally considered safe for healthy people. If you feel dizzy or light-headed, return to normal breathing. Relaxation techniques should not replace medical care or delay seeing a doctor about a health problem.

## When professional support may help
If you often feel short of breath, have chest pain, or have a heart or lung condition, talk to a doctor. If stress or anxiety feels hard to manage even with these skills, consider speaking to a mental health professional.
