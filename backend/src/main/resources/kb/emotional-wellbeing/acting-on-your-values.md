# Doing what matters - acting on your values

## Overview
Stress can narrow life down to just getting through the day. WHO's stress guide encourages **acting on your values**: deciding what kind of person you want to be, even in hard times, and taking small actions in that direction. Values are not goals to tick off; they are qualities you want to bring to what you do, like being caring, honest, brave or hardworking.

## What you can try
1. **Reflect:** What matters most to you? Think about relationships, studies or work, health, community or faith.
2. **Pick a quality:** For example, "I want to be a supportive friend" or "I want to keep learning."
3. **Choose one small action** that fits that value and is realistic today, such as calling a friend, studying for 20 minutes, or helping someone at home.
4. **Notice** how it feels to act in line with what matters, even while stress is present.

## Tips
- Small actions count. Values can guide tiny steps on difficult days.
- You can act on your values while still feeling stressed. The feelings do not need to disappear first.

## Safety and limitations
This is a self-help skill and does not replace professional care. If even small actions feel impossible for a long time, that may be a sign to reach out for support.

## When professional support may help
If you feel stuck, empty, or unable to do things that used to matter to you for weeks, a mental health professional can help.
