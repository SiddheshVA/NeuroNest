# Being kind to yourself

## Overview
Under stress, many people become very hard on themselves: "I'm useless", "I should be coping better". This inner criticism usually adds to the pain rather than helping. WHO's stress guide includes **being kind** as a core skill, both toward yourself and toward others.

## What you can try
- **Notice self-criticism.** Catch the harsh thought and name it: "I'm being hard on myself right now."
- **Talk to yourself like a friend.** Ask, "What would I say to a friend going through this?" Then say something similar to yourself.
- **Use a kind gesture.** Place a hand on your chest or shoulder and take a slow breath.
- **Acknowledge that it is hard.** "This is a difficult time, and it makes sense that I'm struggling."
- **Do one small caring thing** for yourself today: a rest, a walk, a nourishing meal, or a message to someone you trust.
- **Extend kindness to others.** Small acts of kindness can also lift your own mood.

## Safety and limitations
Self-kindness is a helpful habit, but it does not replace professional care. If self-criticism feels constant and overwhelming, it may be part of a bigger struggle that deserves support.

## When professional support may help
If harsh self-talk, low mood or feelings of worthlessness last for weeks or affect daily life, consider speaking with a mental health professional.
