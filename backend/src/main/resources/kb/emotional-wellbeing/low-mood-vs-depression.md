# Low mood and depression - understanding the difference

## Overview
Everyone feels sad, low or "down" sometimes, especially after a disappointment, loss or stressful time. These feelings are a normal part of life and usually ease over days or with support.

Depression is different from ordinary sadness. According to NIMH, it is a common but serious mood condition in which low mood or loss of interest lasts most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks, and affects how a person feels, thinks, sleeps, eats and manages daily activities. Depression can be assessed only by a qualified health professional, and it is treatable.

## Signs worth paying attention to
- Feeling sad, empty or hopeless most of the time
- Losing interest in activities you used to enjoy
- Changes in sleep or appetite
- Low energy or feeling slowed down
- Difficulty concentrating or making decisions

Noticing some of these does not mean someone has depression. It means it may be worth talking to someone.

## What you can try
- Keep some routine: regular sleep, meals and a little daily movement.
- Do one small, manageable activity each day, especially one that used to bring some enjoyment.
- Stay in touch with people you trust, even briefly.
- Be patient and kind with yourself.

## Safety and limitations
NeuroNest does not diagnose depression or any condition. Self-help steps can support wellbeing but are not a substitute for professional care.

## When professional support may help
If low mood or loss of interest lasts two weeks or more, or is affecting your studies, work, relationships or self-care, please talk to a doctor or mental health professional. Effective options include talk therapy and, in some cases, medication prescribed by a doctor.
