# Making room for difficult feelings

## Overview
It is natural to want painful feelings like sadness, fear or anger to go away. But fighting or pushing feelings away often uses a lot of energy and can make them feel bigger. WHO's stress guide describes a skill called **making room**: allowing a difficult feeling to be there, without fighting it, while you carry on with what matters.

Making room does not mean liking the feeling or giving up. It means letting it come and go, like weather passing through.

## What you can try
1. **Notice** the feeling with curiosity. Where do you feel it in your body? Is it heavy, tight, hot?
2. **Name it** gently: "Here is sadness" or "This is fear."
3. **Breathe into it.** Imagine your breath flowing around the feeling, giving it a little more space.
4. **Allow it.** Let it be there for now, without trying to push it out.
5. **Return** your attention to what you are doing or to the people around you.

## Safety and limitations
Making room is a self-help skill and may take practice. It is not a treatment for depression, anxiety or other conditions, and some feelings may be too intense to work with alone.

## When professional support may help
If difficult feelings are constant, very intense, or getting in the way of daily life for weeks, it may help to talk with a counsellor or mental health professional.
