# When to seek professional support

## Overview
Self-care and coping skills help many people through stressful times, but sometimes they are not enough. Reaching out for professional support is a sign of strength, not weakness. Mental health concerns are common and treatable, and getting help early often makes things easier.

## When professional support may help
Consider talking to a doctor, counsellor or mental health professional if, for two weeks or more, you notice:
- Ongoing sadness, worry or irritability
- Trouble sleeping or sleeping much more than usual
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Difficulty getting out of bed or getting through the day
- Trouble concentrating on studies or work
- Losing interest in things you usually enjoy
- Withdrawing from friends and family
- Using alcohol or drugs to cope

You do not need to wait for things to become severe. It is okay to seek help for stress, relationship problems, or simply feeling "not like yourself".

## Getting urgent help
If you are thinking about harming yourself or are in immediate danger, contact emergency services (112 in India) or a crisis helpline right away. The NeuroNest app will show current support contacts.

## Safety and limitations
This list is a general guide, not a diagnostic checklist. Only a qualified professional can assess what someone is experiencing.

## How to take the first step
- Talk to a family doctor or college health service.
- Contact a counsellor at your college or workplace.
- Call a mental health helpline such as Tele MANAS (14416 in India).
- See "Types of mental health support" and "Talking with a health care provider".
