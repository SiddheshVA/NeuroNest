# Talking with a health care provider about mental health

## Overview
Talking to a doctor or counsellor about how you feel can be hard, especially the first time. A little preparation can make the conversation easier and help you get more out of it.

## Before the appointment
- **Write down what you have noticed:** your feelings, sleep, appetite, energy, and when these started.
- **Note anything that seems to make it better or worse.**
- **List any medicines or supplements** you take.
- **Write your questions** so you do not forget them.
- **Consider bringing someone you trust,** if that would help.

## During the appointment
- **Be as honest as you can.** Providers hear about mental health concerns often; there is no need to feel embarrassed.
- **Ask questions,** such as: "What could be causing this?", "What are my options?", "What can I do myself in the meantime?"
- **Ask for explanations** in plain language if something is unclear.
- **Take notes** or ask for written information.

## After the appointment
- Follow the plan you agreed on, and keep a record of how you feel.
- If something is not working, or you have side effects from any treatment, contact your provider rather than stopping on your own.

## Safety and limitations
This is general preparation advice and does not replace guidance from your own provider.

## When professional support may help
It is worth booking an appointment if difficult feelings, sleep problems or stress have lasted two weeks or more, or are affecting your studies, work or relationships. You do not need to be sure what is wrong before you go.
