# Types of mental health support

## Overview
"Getting help" can mean many different things. Knowing the main options can make the first step feel less confusing.

## Common types of support
- **Family doctor or general physician:** A good starting point. They can check for physical causes, offer initial support, and refer you on.
- **Counsellor:** Trained to help with stress, relationships, life changes and emotional difficulties through talking. Many colleges offer free counselling.
- **Clinical psychologist:** Provides assessment and talk therapy, such as cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT).
- **Psychiatrist:** A medical doctor specialising in mental health who can assess, diagnose and prescribe medication when needed.
- **Psychiatric social worker or nurse:** Offers support, counselling and help connecting with services.
- **Helplines and tele-counselling:** Phone or online support, often free and available at any hour.
- **Peer and community support:** Support groups and trusted community members can reduce isolation.

## Talk therapy
Psychotherapy, or talk therapy, helps people understand their thoughts, feelings and behaviours, and learn new ways of coping. Many types exist, and different approaches suit different people.

## Finding the right fit
It is normal to try more than one professional before finding someone you feel comfortable with. It is okay to ask about their training, approach and fees.

## Safety and limitations
This document describes general categories. Roles and titles vary between countries and services. NeuroNest is not a substitute for professional care.

## When professional support may help
If you are unsure where to start, a family doctor, a college counsellor or a helpline such as Tele MANAS (14416 in India) can help you work out which type of support fits your situation.
