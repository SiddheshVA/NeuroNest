# Worry and overthinking - unhooking from difficult thoughts

## Overview
When people are stressed, their minds often produce a stream of difficult thoughts: worries about the future, replays of the past, or harsh judgements. It is easy to get "hooked" by these thoughts, so that they pull attention away from what is happening now and from what matters.

WHO's stress guide describes a skill called **unhooking**. The idea is not to argue with or push away thoughts, but to notice them and loosen their grip.

## What you can try
1. **Notice** that a difficult thought or feeling is present.
2. **Name it** in a simple way, for example: "Here is a worrying thought" or "I am noticing the thought that I will fail."
3. **Refocus** on what you are doing right now: the task in front of you, the person you are with, or your surroundings.

Some people find it useful to imagine thoughts as words passing by, like cars on a road or leaves on a stream, without needing to follow each one.

## Safety and limitations
Unhooking is a self-help skill. It may take practice, and it does not remove worries completely. It is not a treatment for anxiety disorders or other conditions.

## When professional support may help
If worry feels constant, hard to control, or is affecting sleep, studies, work or relationships for weeks, it may help to speak with a counsellor or mental health professional.
