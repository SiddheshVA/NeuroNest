# Coping in an anxious moment

## Overview
When anxiety rises quickly, the body can feel like it is in alarm mode: a fast heartbeat, quick shallow breathing, tight muscles and racing thoughts. These feelings are uncomfortable but usually pass. Simple skills that slow the body down can help the wave feel more manageable.

## What you can try
1. **Pause and name it.** Quietly say to yourself, "I am feeling anxious right now. This feeling will pass."
2. **Slow your breath.** Breathe in gently through your nose, then breathe out slowly, making the out-breath a little longer than the in-breath. Repeat for a minute or two.
3. **Ground yourself.** Press your feet into the floor and notice a few things you can see and hear around you.
4. **Relax one area.** Drop your shoulders, unclench your jaw and loosen your hands.
5. **Choose one small next step,** such as drinking water, stepping outside, or messaging someone you trust.

## Safety and limitations
These skills may help some people feel calmer, but they do not work the same for everyone and do not treat an anxiety disorder. If you have a heart, breathing or other medical condition, or if symptoms like chest pain or fainting occur, seek medical attention rather than assuming it is anxiety.

## When professional support may help
If anxious moments happen often, feel overwhelming, or lead you to avoid places and activities, consider talking with a doctor or mental health professional.
