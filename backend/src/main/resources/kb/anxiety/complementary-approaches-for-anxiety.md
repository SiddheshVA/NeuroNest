# Complementary approaches for anxiety - what research says

## Overview
Many people are curious whether practices like meditation or relaxation can help with anxiety. NCCIH, part of the US National Institutes of Health, summarises the research.

## Key points from the research
- **Mindfulness meditation:** Reviews of many studies suggest mindfulness programs may help reduce anxiety for some people, with moderate evidence overall. One analysis found meditation may be slightly more helpful than relaxation therapies for anxiety.
- **Relaxation techniques:** These may ease anxiety for people with long-term medical problems or those facing medical procedures. For generalised anxiety, research suggests that conventional talk therapy, such as cognitive behavioural therapy, may be more effective over the long term than relaxation alone.
- **Safety:** Meditation and relaxation techniques are generally considered safe for healthy people. People with serious physical or mental health problems are advised to discuss them with a health care provider.

## What this means in practice
These practices can be a helpful part of looking after yourself. They work best as one tool among several, such as good sleep, activity, social support and, where needed, professional care.

## Safety and limitations
Research findings describe averages across groups of people; they cannot predict how a practice will work for one person. Supplements and herbal products marketed for anxiety are not covered here and can have side effects or interactions, so talk to a doctor before using them.

## When professional support may help
If anxiety is persistent or interfering with daily life, a health professional can discuss options with good evidence, including talk therapy.
