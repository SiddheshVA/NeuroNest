# Grounding when emotions feel overwhelming

## Overview
When emotions are very strong, it can be hard to think, decide or act in ways that help. WHO's stress guide suggests a short grounding sequence that can be done in a minute or two, almost anywhere.

## How to do it
1. **Slow down.** Breathe out slowly and let your shoulders drop.
2. **Notice your thoughts and feelings.** Quietly acknowledge them: "I'm noticing anger" or "I'm having the thought that this is too much."
3. **Connect with your body.** Push your feet into the floor, straighten your back, press your palms together, or stretch slowly.
4. **Engage with the world around you.** Look around and notice what you can see and hear. Notice where you are and what you are doing.
5. **Repeat** steps 2-4 a few times, slowly, until you feel a little more steady.

## After grounding
Once you feel steadier, ask: "What is one small helpful thing I can do next?" It might be drinking water, stepping outside, or talking to someone.

## Safety and limitations
Grounding can help people stay steady but does not remove problems or painful feelings. It is a self-help skill, not a treatment. If you feel unsafe or in crisis, contact emergency services or a crisis helpline right away; the NeuroNest app will show support options.

## When professional support may help
If overwhelming emotions happen often, last a long time, or lead to actions you regret, a mental health professional can help.
