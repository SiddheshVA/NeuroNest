# The 5-4-3-2-1 senses exercise

## Overview
This is a simple grounding exercise that uses your five senses to bring attention back to the here and now. It can be done anywhere, quietly, in about two to three minutes.

## How to do it
Take a slow breath, then notice:
- **5 things you can see** - for example, a window, a cup, the colour of a wall.
- **4 things you can feel** - your feet on the floor, your clothes on your skin, the chair under you.
- **3 things you can hear** - traffic, a fan, birds, your own breathing.
- **2 things you can smell** - or two smells you like, if nothing is noticeable.
- **1 thing you can taste** - or one thing you are grateful for.

Name each one silently or out loud. Take your time with each.

## Tips
- There is no right answer. Small details count.
- If you lose your place, just start again with whatever sense you remember.
- Combine it with slow breathing for extra calm.

## Safety and limitations
This exercise is a commonly used grounding technique written by the NeuroNest team, inspired by WHO's grounding skill rather than copied from a single official source. It may help some people feel steadier, but it does not treat any condition.

## When professional support may help
If you often need grounding to get through the day, or if you feel disconnected from your surroundings regularly, consider speaking with a mental health professional.
