# What grounding is

## Overview
Strong emotions can feel like being caught in a storm: thoughts race, feelings rush in, and it is hard to think clearly. WHO's stress guide describes **grounding** as a way to steady yourself during these moments, like a boat dropping an anchor.

Grounding does not make the storm disappear. Instead, it helps you stay steady while it passes, so you can choose what to do next.

## How grounding works
Grounding brings attention back to the present moment using:
- **Your body:** feeling your feet on the floor, pressing your hands together, noticing your posture.
- **Your senses:** looking at objects nearby, listening to sounds, noticing temperature or textures.
- **Your breath:** slowing it down gently.

## When people use it
- During a wave of stress, anger or worry
- When feeling overwhelmed or "spaced out"
- Before a difficult conversation or task
- When thoughts keep pulling toward the past or the future

## Safety and limitations
Grounding is a self-help skill and may take practice. It is not a treatment for any mental health condition. If grounding exercises feel distressing, stop and try something else.

## When professional support may help
If you often feel overwhelmed, disconnected from yourself or your surroundings, or flooded by difficult memories, a mental health professional can help you understand and manage this.
