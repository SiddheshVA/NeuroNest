# Staying connected with others

## Overview
Feeling connected to other people is one of the strongest supports for mental wellbeing. Friends, family, classmates and community can offer emotional support, practical help and a sense of belonging. When people are stressed or low, they often pull away from others, which can make things feel heavier.

## What you can try
- **Start small.** A short message, a voice note or a quick call counts.
- **Schedule time** with someone you feel comfortable with, such as a walk, a meal or studying together.
- **Join something.** A club, sports team, volunteer group, faith community or online interest group can create regular contact.
- **Be honest with someone you trust.** Saying "I've been having a tough week" can open the door to support.
- **Offer support too.** Being kind and helpful to others can strengthen connection and lift your own mood.
- **Notice who leaves you feeling better,** and spend more time with them.

## If you feel lonely
Loneliness is common, especially after moving for studies or work. It does not mean something is wrong with you. Building connection takes time; small, regular steps tend to help most.

## Safety and limitations
This is general guidance. If relationships feel unsafe, stressful or harmful, connection with those people may not be helpful, and support from a trusted person or professional may be needed.

## When professional support may help
If loneliness or withdrawal from others lasts for weeks, or comes with low mood or hopelessness, consider talking to a counsellor or mental health professional.
